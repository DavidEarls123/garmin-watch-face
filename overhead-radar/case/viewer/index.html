Assembly

Steps 1–3 test everything on the bench before it goes into the case. That way, if something doesn't work, you're not unscrewing a finished build.

  1. Prepare the microSD card

    Need: microSD card, a computer

    • Install Raspberry Pi Imager on your computer. Choose Raspberry Pi 5 → Raspberry Pi OS (64-bit), the version with the desktop.
    • In the settings (the cog, or Edit settings), set a username and password and your Wi-Fi, and tick Enable SSH.
    • Write the card, then put it in the Pi.

  2. Fit the Active Cooler and bench test the screen

    Need: Pi 5, Active Cooler, screen, micro-HDMI cable, the screen's USB cable, power supply

    • Peel the film off the cooler's thermal pads. Push its two spring pins into the matching holes on the Pi until they click, then plug the fan lead into the FAN socket.
    • Connect the micro-HDMI cable to HDMI 0, the port next to the USB-C power socket. Connect the screen's USB cable to a Pi USB port; this carries touch and power for the screen.
    • Plug in the power supply. The desktop should appear on the round screen and respond to touch.
    • Put a small piece of tape on the screen's edge that is the top of the picture. You'll need it in step 6.
    • If the picture is blank or the wrong size, follow the "5inch 1080x1080 LCD" page on the Waveshare wiki for the lines to add to config.txt.

  3. Install the radar software

    Need: the Pi on Wi-Fi, the RTL-SDR dongle, the USB extension, the antenna

    • Plug the dongle into the USB extension and the extension into a Pi USB port. Screw the antenna's SMA plug onto the dongle; you don't need the MCX adapter.
    • Open a terminal on the Pi and fetch the project. The work is on its own branch until you merge it:
    git clone -b claude/garmin-forerunner-watchface-options-esgt9b https://github.com/davidearls123/garmin-watch-face.git
cd garmin-watch-face/overhead-radar
sudo ./tools/setup-pi.sh --with-receiver --lat 53.2707 --lon -9.0568 --name Galway
    • If the repository is private, Git will ask you to sign in. Use your GitHub username and a personal access token as the password.
    • When it finishes, run sudo reboot. The radar should open full screen. Under the clock it should say RECEIVER+ADSBLOL.
    • Check: tap a plane to see its details, use + and − to zoom, use the pin button to change place, and use ✈ to track a flight.

  4. Shut down and unplug everything

    Run sudo shutdown now, wait for the green light to stop flashing, then unplug the power and the cables.

  5. Pad the retaining ring

    Need: retaining ring, foam tape

    • Stick the foam tape around the flat face of the ring, close to its inner edge, so it covers the 3 mm band that presses on the back edge of the screen. Butt the ends together; don't overlap them.
    • Leave the green backing film on the exposed side. The foam is then a cushion, not glue, and the screen can come out again.

  6. Put the screen into the bezel

    Need: bezel, screen, retaining ring, 4 × M2.5 × 8

    • Lay the bezel face-down on a soft cloth. Wipe the glass, then drop the screen face-down into the pocket.
    • Turn it so the taped top edge is at the top of the bezel. For the instrument style, the top is opposite the stand window. For the puck, the top is opposite the flat underside.
    • Lay the ring over the back of the screen, foam down, and screw it in with the four M2.5 × 8. Tighten them in a cross pattern until they just stop. The glass only needs holding, so don't overtighten.

  7. Mount the Pi in the box

    Need: box or body, Pi, 4 × M2.5 × 6 or 8

    • Screw the Pi onto the four standoffs, cooler facing up (towards the screen). The screws cut their own thread the first time, so turn firmly but stop when snug.
    • From outside, feed the power supply's USB-C plug in through the large 16 mm hole and plug it into the Pi.

  8. Fit the dongle and the antenna lead

    Need: dongle, USB extension, antenna, grommet, foam tape

    • Plug the USB extension into the Pi and the dongle into the extension. Stick the dongle to the floor with a strip of foam tape, as far from the Pi as it will go.
    • From outside, push the antenna's SMA plug in through the 13 mm port and screw it onto the dongle.
    • Click the antenna cable sideways into the grommet's slot, then press the grommet into the port from outside until it snaps.

  9. Connect the screen and close the case

    Need: micro-HDMI cable, the screen's USB cable, 4 × M3 × 12/16 (puck: 3)

    • Connect the micro-HDMI (Pi HDMI 0) and the screen's USB cable between the bezel assembly and the Pi. Tuck the cables down beside the Pi so they don't press on the screen.
    • Fit the bezel onto the box with the top edges matching. Drive the M3 button-head screws into the corner holes until the heads sit in their recesses.

  10. Stand it up and place the antenna

    • Instrument: sit the box in the stand with its underside on the shelf and its back against the rest. The power and antenna leads come out through the window in the rest.
    • Puck: stand it on its flat underside. It leans back on its own.
    • Put the antenna upright in a window, as high as you can and away from the Pi. The magnetic base works best on a metal plate, such as a biscuit-tin lid.

  11. Power on and finish setup

    • Plug in the power. The radar starts by itself after about a minute.
    • Open the pin button, choose Add a place…, and save your house and the airfields you use.
    • After a day or two of running, the receiver can tune its gain. Plug in a keyboard, or connect over SSH, and run sudo readsb-gain autogain.

If something's not right

Screen stays black

Use HDMI 0, the port next to the power socket. Check the screen's USB cable is plugged in; it powers the screen. Then add the Waveshare config.txt lines.

Touch doesn't respond

The touch signal travels over the screen's USB cable, so make sure that cable goes to the Pi and not just to a charger.

Picture is upside down or sideways

Rotate it in the Pi's Screen Configuration tool, or turn the screen in the bezel (steps 5–6).

Says ADSBLOL only, no RECEIVER

Run lsusb; it should list a Realtek RTL2838 device. Then run journalctl -u readsb -n 50 to see the receiver's log.

Radar doesn't open at boot

Run journalctl -u overhead-radar -n 50. Opening http://localhost:8000 in a browser tells you whether the server is running.

Few planes on the receiver

Move the antenna higher and nearer the window, away from the Pi, and stand it on metal. Run autogain after a day.
